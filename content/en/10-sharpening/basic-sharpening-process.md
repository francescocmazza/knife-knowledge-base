# A Practical Sharpening Process

This process is intended as a practical introduction for double-bevel kitchen knives. Single-bevel knives require a different method.

## 1. Inspect the knife

Check for:

- chips;
- rolled sections;
- uneven bevels;
- the existing sharpening angle;
- excessive thickness behind the edge.

Choose the stone according to the work required. Very dull or damaged edges may need a coarse stone. Routine sharpening commonly starts with a medium stone.

## 2. Prepare the stone

Most absorbent water stones should be soaked until bubbling substantially stops. Special and splash-and-go stones must be prepared according to the manufacturer's instructions.

Secure the stone on a stable, non-slip surface.

## 3. Follow the existing bevel

A 15-degree angle per side is a useful starting reference for many thin double-bevel kitchen knives, but it is not universal.

The best training rule is:

> Match the existing bevel unless you deliberately intend to change the edge geometry.

## 4. Work in controlled sections

Place the supporting fingers close to the edge over the area being sharpened. Use consistent strokes and move progressively from heel to tip.

A set number of strokes can help an inexperienced user maintain rhythm, but it is not the completion criterion.

## 5. Form a light continuous burr

Continue until a small burr can be felt along the full length of the opposite side. Where the burr is missing, work that area again with fewer controlled strokes.

## 6. Repeat on the second side

Turn the knife over and repeat until the burr moves consistently to the first side.

## 7. Reduce pressure and alternate

Use lighter pressure and fewer strokes. Alternate sides to weaken, centre, and remove the burr without building a large wire edge.

Pressure may be applied differently according to the chosen sharpening method. Edge-leading, edge-trailing, and mixed techniques can all be valid when controlled properly.

## 8. Refine if needed

Move to a finer stone when the intended edge needs additional refinement or polishing. A higher grit is not automatically better for every kitchen task; some foods benefit from a more aggressive medium-grit finish.

## 9. Check the result

Use a combination of:

- visual and tactile burr inspection;
- a controlled paper-cutting test;
- an appropriate food test;
- observation of whether the knife initiates and continues the cut cleanly.

Shaving hair is not required for normal kitchen training.

## 10. Clean, dry, and maintain the stone

Rinse the blade and stone, dry the knife immediately, and let the stone dry fully. Check stone flatness and use a proper flattening stone or lapping plate when necessary.

## Customer-facing summary

> Do not sharpen by counting strokes alone. Follow the bevel, form a light continuous burr, repeat on the other side, then reduce pressure and remove the burr cleanly.
