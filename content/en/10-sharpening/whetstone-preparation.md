# Preparing Water Stones

Most common absorbent water stones should be soaked before use. A practical rule is to immerse the stone until the stream of air bubbles has substantially stopped, showing that the pores have absorbed the water they need.

## The manufacturer always has priority

The soaking rule does not apply to every stone.

Special stones may require:

- only a splash of water on the surface;
- a very short soak;
- no soaking at all;
- a specific lubricant other than water.

Dense ceramic, resin-bonded, diamond, and splash-and-go stones can behave very differently from traditional porous water stones. Long soaking may weaken, soften, crack, or damage some products.

## Recommended training wording

> Most traditional absorbent water stones should be soaked until bubbling substantially stops. Always follow the manufacturer's instructions, because special and splash-and-go stones may need only surface wetting or no soaking.

## During sharpening

- Keep the working surface adequately wet.
- Do not allow a water stone to dry during use.
- Add water as needed to control friction and move swarf.
- Do not assume that all slurry must always be preserved or always be washed away; the best approach depends on the stone and sharpening stage.

## After use

- Rinse away loose residue.
- Clean the surface according to the manufacturer's guidance.
- Allow the stone to air-dry completely in a ventilated place.
- Avoid direct heat, intense sun, or storage while wet.
- Check flatness regularly.
