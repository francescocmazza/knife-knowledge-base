# Knife Types and Cutting Styles

Knife names describe a design tradition, but the exact profile, thickness, balance, and grind of the individual model determine how it feels in use.

## General-purpose knives

| Type | Main character | Typical cutting style |
|---|---|---|
| **Gyuto** | Japanese chef's knife, available in many lengths and profiles | Slicing, push cutting, chopping, and—when the belly is curved enough—rocking |
| **Santoku** | Compact all-purpose knife, usually tall and easy to control | Push cutting, chopping, and moderate rocking depending on profile |
| **Bunka** | Santoku-like versatility with a K-tip and commonly straighter edge | Push cutting, chopping, and detailed tip work; usually less suited to deep rocking |
| **Chinese chef's knife / cai dao** | Tall, versatile blade used for vegetables, meat, and general preparation | Chopping, slicing, scooping with the blade face; thin models are not bone cleavers |

## Vegetable knives

| Type | Main character | Important note |
|---|---|---|
| **Nakiri** | Thin, tall, double-bevel vegetable knife with a relatively straight edge | Designed for vegetables, not bones |
| **Usuba** | Traditional single-bevel vegetable knife | Supports peeling and precise thin cuts; requires greater skill |

## Slicers and fish knives

| Type | Main character | Important note |
|---|---|---|
| **Sujihiki** | Long double-bevel slicer for clean cuts through boneless proteins | Excellent for meat and also usable for many fish preparations |
| **Yanagiba** | Long traditional single-bevel slicer | Optimized for long, clean slices of raw fish and presentation work |
| **Fillet knife** | Narrow, often flexible blade | Designed to follow bones and contours; not the same as a sujihiki or yanagiba |

## Butchery and boning

| Type | Main character | Important note |
|---|---|---|
| **Deba** | Thick single-bevel knife for breaking down fish | Suitable for fish collars, joints, and appropriate bones with correct technique; not a universal bone cleaver |
| **Honesuki** | Narrow, pointed boning knife commonly used for poultry | Designed to work around joints and small bones rather than chop through heavy bone |
| **Western boning knife** | Narrow blade, sometimes flexible | Useful for trimming and following bone contours |
| **Chopper / bone cleaver** | Heavy, robust blade | Built for forceful work that would damage a thin Chinese chef's knife |

## Small knives

| Type | Main character | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| **Petty / utility** | Small versatile knife used on or above the board | Fruit, small vegetables, cheese, meat trimming, garlic, and quick preparation |
| **Paring knife** | Very small knife for hand work | Peeling, coring, turning, and decorative work |

## Serrated knives

A bread knife starts several small cuts through a resistant crust while reducing pressure on the soft interior. It is also useful for cakes, delicate baked goods, and some soft-skinned foods.

## Profile matters more than the label

- A curved belly supports rocking.
- A flatter edge supports push cutting and chopping.
- A fine K-tip improves detail work but can be more delicate.
- Thickness behind the edge affects penetration more than the knife's category name alone.
