# Single and Double Bevels

## Double bevel

A double-bevel knife is sharpened from both sides. When the geometry and handle are symmetrical, it can normally be used comfortably in either hand.

Some double-bevel knives use an asymmetrical edge, such as a stronger bias toward one side. These can still be usable by both hands, but may steer differently and may require product-specific sharpening guidance.

## Single bevel

A traditional single-bevel knife has a main bevel on one side and a flat or slightly concave reverse side. This brings the cutting edge close to one face of the blade and supports very precise slicing, peeling, and presentation work.

Common examples include traditional yanagiba, deba, and usuba knives.

## Right- and left-handed versions

Single-bevel knives are designed and optimized for a particular hand. The intended orientation affects steering, food release, and the direction in which the cut surface is separated.

However, a knife made for the opposite hand is not necessarily impossible to use. An experienced user can often compensate by changing:

- the knife's orientation;
- body position;
- the position of the ingredient;
- the direction of work;
- wrist angle and pressure.

In many operations this can produce a result that is close to the intended-hand version. The correct handed version remains easier, more natural, and more repeatable for most users.

## Customer-facing summary

> A single-bevel knife is optimized for one hand, but technique and working position can compensate in many tasks. The matching version is the easiest choice; the opposite version is a skill challenge, not an absolute impossibility.
