# Safe Use and Carrying

A sharp knife can be easier to control because it requires less force, but it remains dangerous when used carelessly. Safety comes from a sharp edge, a suitable knife, stable working conditions, and deliberate technique.

## Basic use

- Use a stable wooden or suitable plastic cutting board.
- Keep the working area clear and dry.
- Use the correct knife for the task.
- Avoid glass, ceramic, stone, and metal cutting surfaces.
- Do not twist a thin edge inside hard food.
- Do not use a kitchen knife as a pry bar, screwdriver, can opener, or scraper.
- Avoid frozen food and hard bones unless the knife is specifically designed for them.
- Hand wash and dry the knife promptly.

## Moving with a knife

When moving a short distance in a kitchen:

1. Hold the handle firmly.
2. Keep the knife close to your side.
3. Point the tip toward the floor.
4. Keep the cutting edge facing backward or otherwise away from the body.
5. Keep the arm still rather than swinging it.
6. Clearly warn nearby people that you are passing with a knife.
7. Never run.

Do not cover the tip with the free hand and do not carry the blade pointing upward.

For transport beyond the immediate workstation, use a blade guard, saya, case, or knife roll.

## Customer-facing summary

> Carry a knife low and still: tip down, edge away, arm close to the body, and always warn people nearby.
