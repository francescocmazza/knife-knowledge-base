# Structure and Visual Audit

Reviewed: 2026-08-04  
Scope: complete English source-of-truth site and the permitted legacy training slides supplied for this project.

## Editorial conclusion

The current knowledge base is technically coherent, practical and unusually careful about avoiding misleading absolutes. Its main weakness is not accuracy but **learning sequence and density**.

The original navigation began with metallurgy. For a new reader, geometry and cutting movement are easier to understand because they can be seen immediately. The site navigation and home-page reading path have therefore been reordered as follows:

1. **How knives cut** — bevels, profiles and cutting movements;
2. **Materials and performance** — five dimensions, alloying and blade construction;
3. **Use and safety**;
4. **Sharpening** — stones, burr and complete process.

This sequence gives the reader a visible physical model before introducing hardness, carbides and alloy composition.

## Structural improvements still recommended

### Add one foundational anatomy chapter

A short chapter called **How a Kitchen Knife Cuts** should eventually introduce:

- spine, face, heel, belly, tip and apex;
- primary grind, bevel and edge bevel;
- thickness behind the edge;
- blade height, curvature and distal taper;
- wedging, steering and food separation.

Several existing chapters explain these ideas independently. A shared visual foundation would reduce repetition.

### Split the knife-types chapter

`Knife Types and Cutting Styles` is useful but very long. It should become an overview plus four linked chapters:

1. general-purpose and vegetable knives;
2. slicers and fish knives;
3. boning knives, Chinese knives and cleavers;
4. small and serrated knives.

The overview should retain the movement-first selector and comparison tables.

### Group the sharpening process into phases

The detailed sharpening chapter currently uses seventeen steps. The information is good, but the reader must remember too many separate stages. It should be presented visually as six phases:

1. inspect and choose the abrasive;
2. prepare the stone and establish the angle;
3. sharpen the first side to a continuous burr;
4. repeat on the second side;
5. refine and deburr;
6. inspect, test and clean.

The existing detailed steps can remain beneath those phase headings.

### Reduce repeated caveats

Several pages repeat variations of:

- one feature does not determine the whole knife;
- more is not automatically better;
- the final result depends on heat treatment and geometry.

These principles are essential, but they should be stated strongly on the home page and the future anatomy chapter, then repeated only where the reader could otherwise reach a specific false conclusion.

### Move background material out of the main learning flow

The historical wootz section is valuable but interrupts the practical comparison between clad and full Damascus. It should become a clearly marked background box or appear after the practical evaluation section.

Administrative explanations about language, licensing and contributions belong primarily on the home page, repository README or an About page rather than inside technical chapters.

## Consistent article pattern

Future revisions should generally use this order:

1. **Opening question or practical problem**;
2. **visual overview**;
3. **plain-language explanation**;
4. **why it matters in use**;
5. **important limits or misunderstandings**;
6. **customer-facing summary**.

Long pages should also include an **At a glance** box near the top.

## Rules for adapting the supplied material

- Do not publish full legacy slides when only the photograph or diagram is needed.
- Crop out outdated pricing, brand comparisons, steel specifications and unsupported superlatives.
- Translate or redraw Italian diagrams rather than adding multilingual text directly inside one image.
- Treat legacy product photography as illustrative, not as a current catalogue claim.
- Confirm that embedded photographs may be republished publicly; if their rights are uncertain, replace them with an original or properly licensed image.
- Do not use images from the confidential Musashi manual.
- State separately whether an image is covered by the repository’s CC licence; third-party or limited-permission images should be explicitly excluded.
