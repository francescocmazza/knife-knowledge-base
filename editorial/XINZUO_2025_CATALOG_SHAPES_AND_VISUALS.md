# 2025 Xinzuo Catalog — Knife Shapes and Visual Inventory

Reviewed: 2026-08-04  
Source: `2025 Xinzuo Catalog V9.pdf`, 61 PDF pages / 113 printed catalog pages.

This inventory records the terminology and visuals present in the catalog. It does **not** automatically convert every catalog label or commercial statement into general knowledge-base terminology. Some names are established knife categories; others are product-family or marketing labels that require explanation before public educational use.

## 2. Additional names found in product-series pages

The product pages add several labels that are not present in the main shape chart.

### Established or useful additional categories

- **Butter Knife**;
- **Fillet Knife**;
- **Honesuki Knife**;
- **Gyuto Knife**;
- **Chinese Slicing Knife**;
- **Chopper Knife** or **Chopper**;
- **Granton Edge Chef Knife**.

### Catalog-specific or commercially defined names

- **Viking Knife**;
- **Ultimate Utility Knife**.

These two names should not be treated as universal taxonomy without a short explanation of the exact profile and intended work.

### Terminology variants in the catalog

- `Frozen Knife` and `Frozen Food Knife` refer to the same commercial family;
- `Carving Knife`, `Roast Carving Knife`, `Granton Carving Knife` and `Ham Knife` overlap as long slicing forms but should remain distinct where their geometry differs;
- `Cleaver`, `Chinese Cleaver`, `Bone Chopper` and `Chopper` must not be collapsed into one category without explaining thickness, weight and intended impact work;
- `Chef Knife` and `Gyuto Knife` both appear and should remain separate source labels even though their modern functions may overlap;
- `Sashimi Knife` is used as a broad catalog label for long single-bevel fish slicers and should be connected carefully to the more specific educational vocabulary used in the knowledge base.

## 6. Important editorial conflicts and cautions

### Frozen-food contradiction

The blade-shape chart says the bread knife can be used for frozen food, while the care page says frozen food must be completely thawed before cutting with a sharp knife. The knowledge base should follow the safer rule and must not reproduce the first claim as general advice.

### Sharpening rules

The catalog presents a fixed five-minute soak, strongly advises a 15-degree guide and gives fixed grit/time instructions. These should not replace the approved knowledge-base guidance that preparation depends on the stone, 15 degrees is only a common reference and the burr/edge condition—not a universal time or stroke count—determines completion.

### Product names versus universal categories

`Viking Knife`, `Ultimate Utility Knife` and some uses of `Sashimi Knife` are catalog terminology. They should be defined by visible geometry and intended use before being presented as general categories.

### Image reuse

The catalog is a current Xinzuo commercial source and is substantially more suitable than the old training slides for current product photography. Public reuse still requires confirmation that the repository may publish the individual catalog photographs. Until that is confirmed, the images remain approved **candidates**, not automatically CC-licensed assets.

### Recommended publication method

- use the catalog to identify and crop authorized current photographs;
- redraw taxonomy, force, geometry and process diagrams as original assets;
- remove model numbers, outdated claims and unrelated product data from educational crops;
- mark product examples as examples rather than universal definitions;
- state explicitly when an image is not covered by the repository's general CC BY-NC-SA licence.
